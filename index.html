📘 1. Key Concepts — Participle Rules

Os particípios são adjetivos formados a partir de verbos. A escolha entre -ed e -ing depende de quem causa e quem sente.

✅ Affirmation — Correct Use A2
❓ Which participle should we use to describe a feeling?
✅ Use -ed to describe a feeling or state: excited (animado), interested (interessado), bored (entediado).
Examples: She is excited. · He is interested in art. · I am bored.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 excited → animado 🔊 interested → interessado 🔊 bored → entediado
❌ Negation — Common Mistakes A2
❓ Which participle should we use to describe a thing?
✅ Use -ing to describe a characteristic or cause: boring (entediante), exciting (empolgante), confusing (confuso — que causa confusão).
Examples: The movie is boring. · The news is exciting. · The instructions are confusing.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 boring → entediante 🔊 exciting → empolgante 🔊 confusing → confuso
❓ Doubt — How to Choose B1
❓ How do we differentiate -ed from -ing?
✅ Rule: -ed describes the person (feeling); -ing describes the thing (cause).
Pairs: frightened (assustado) / frightening (assustador) · tired (cansado) / tiring (cansativo)
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 frightened → assustado 🔊 frightening → assustador 🔊 tired → cansado 🔊 tiring → cansativo
🔄 Frequency — Most Common A2–B1
❓ Which participles are most used in everyday English?
✅ The most frequent pairs are interested/interesting, tired/tiring, excited/exciting.
Examples: I am interested. · The class is interesting. · She is tired. · The work is tiring.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 interesting → interessante 🔊 exciting → empolgante
⚡ Intensity — Emphasis B1
❓ How do we add emphasis to participles?
✅ Use absolutely, really, very before the participle: absolutely fascinating, really tired, very exciting.
Examples: The lecture was absolutely fascinating. · I am really tired. · The trip was very exciting.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 absolutely fascinating → absolutamente fascinante 🔊 really tired → realmente cansado 🔊 very exciting → muito empolgante
📍 Position in the Sentence B1
❓ Where do we place the participle in the sentence?
✅ Attributive: before the noun — an exciting movie. Predicative: after the verb — the movie is exciting.
Examples: Attributive: an exciting movie · a confusing situation. · Predicative: the movie is exciting · the situation is confusing.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 attributive → atributivo 🔊 predicative → predicativo
🎯 Mode — Correct Form A2–B1
❓ What is the correct participle form?
✅ Regular: walk → walked / walking. Irregular: write → written / writing · run → run / running.
Examples: walk → walked / walking · write → written / writing · run → run / running.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 walked → caminhou 🔊 written → escrito 🔊 running → correndo
⏰ Time — Present vs Past B1–B2
❓ Which participle do we use for each tense?
✅ Present Continuous: I am reading. Present Perfect: I have read. Past Continuous: I was reading.
Examples: I am reading. · I have read. · I was reading.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 reading → lendo 🔊 read → lido

📗 2. Past Participle Adjectives (-ed)

Adjetivos formados a partir do particípio passado de verbos. Eles descrevem como uma pessoa se sente ou o estado de algo.

📘 What are Past Participle Adjectives? A2
❓ What are past participle adjectives?
✅ Formed by adding -ed to regular verbs or using the 3rd form of irregular verbs. They describe feelings or states resulting from an action.
Examples: I am interested in learning English. · He seemed tired after work. · The children were excited about the trip.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 interested → interessado 🔊 tired → cansado 🔊 excited → animado
🔧 Formation of -ed Adjectives A2–B1
❓ How do we form adjectives with -ed?
✅ Regular: interest → interested · surprise → surprised. Irregular: break → broken · write → written. Watch spelling: stop → stopped · worry → worried.
Examples: interested, surprised, broken, written, stopped, worried.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 broken → quebrado 🔊 written → escrito 🔊 worried → preocupado
📝 Use of -ed Adjectives A2–B1
❓ How and when do we use -ed adjectives?
✅ Structure: Subject + be/get/become/feel + -ed adjective. Examples: She is bored with the movie. The window was broken by the storm.
Examples: She is bored with the movie. · He seemed tired after work. · The children were excited about the trip.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 bored → entediado 🔊 seemed → pareceu 🔊 excited → animado
📚 Common -ed Vocabulary A2
❓ Which -ed adjectives are most common?
✅ These adjectives describe common feelings. Use them with be, feel, seem, become. Examples: I'm tired today. / She seems worried.
Pairs to memorize: bored/boring · interested/interesting · excited/exciting · tired/tiring · surprised/surprising · confused/confusing · worried/worrying · disappointed/disappointing.
📚 Key Vocabulary: 🔊 surprised → surpreso 🔊 confused → confuso 🔊 worried → preocupado 🔊 disappointed → decepcionado

🔄 3. -ed vs -ing — The Essential Difference

A diferença entre -ed e -ing é uma das mais importantes em inglês. Veja os pares e pratique com áudio US/UK.

Feeling (-ed)Cause (-ing)ExampleÁudio
interested (interessado) interesting (interessante) I am interested in science. Science is interesting.
bored (entediado) boring (entediante) I am bored. The movie is boring.
excited (animado) exciting (empolgante) She is excited. The news is exciting.
tired (cansado) tiring (cansativo) He is tired. The work is tiring.
surprised (surpreso) surprising (surpreendente) We were surprised. The news was surprising.
confused (confuso) confusing (confuso — que causa confusão) I am confused. The instructions are confusing.
frightened (assustado) frightening (assustador) The child was frightened. The film was frightening.
worried (preocupado) worrying (preocupante) I am worried. The situation is worrying.
disappointed (decepcionado) disappointing (decepcionante) She was disappointed. The result was disappointing.
💡 Regra de ouro: -ed descreve quem sente (pessoa) — I am bored. -ing descreve o que causa o sentimento (coisa/situação) — The movie is boring.

🎧 6. Listening Practice — A Day at Work

Ouça o texto e responda às perguntas. Ele usa particípios -ed e -ing em contexto natural.

Hi, my name is Laura. Yesterday was a very tiring day at work. I was really tired by the end of the afternoon. My manager gave us a confusing presentation — the instructions were not clear, so we all felt confused. But the project itself is exciting! I am interested in the new challenge, and my colleagues are excited too. One of them was surprised by the news, because the company has changed a lot. The new office is amazing — it is a fascinating place. I am not bored at all. At the end of the day, I was disappointed because I had lost my keys, but I found them later. Overall, it was an interesting day.
Olá, meu nome é Laura. Ontem foi um dia muito cansativo no trabalho. Eu estava realmente cansada ao final da tarde. Meu gerente nos deu uma apresentação confusa — as instruções não eram claras, então todos nos sentimos confusos. Mas o projeto em si é empolgante! Estou interessada no novo desafio, e meus colegas estão animados também. Um deles ficou surpreso com a notícia, porque a empresa mudou muito. O novo escritório é incrível — é um lugar fascinante. Não estou nem um pouco entediada. No final do dia, fiquei decepcionada porque havia perdido minhas chaves, mas as encontrei depois. No geral, foi um dia interessante.
